Sidecar — icon concepts

Four directions, each rendered at the sizes that actually matter — 256 for the installer/about box, down to 16 for the tray, and once against a light desktop since tray icons don't always sit on dark.

A

Dock

The literal meaning of "sidecar" in software: a small process attached to a bigger one it doesn't own. An outlined square stands for Antigravity — present, but not Sidecar's to fill — with a solid clay square docked at its corner, slightly overlapping. It's the only concept of the four that says what the app is rather than just what color it is.

B

Twin Pane

Two overlapping rounded panes of near-equal size — a nod to the app's real duality (reading one session, sending to another) rather than the host/guest relationship of A. Reads a little more like a generic "windows" or "layers" icon at small sizes, which is the trade-off against A's specificity.

Mark (current, refined)

Keeps what's already working — a single solid clay square is legible and calm at every size — but replaces the dark corner dot with a small notch cut from the corner, like a bite taken out where a companion window would dock. Cheapest change from what's shipping now, and the least distinctive of the four.

D

Read / Send

Built from the app's own vocabulary — the eye and arrow marks used throughout the UI for "what you're reading" and "what you're sending to." An arrow crosses in front of a simplified eye. The most literal tie to the product, but eyes are notoriously hard to keep legible under about 24px — worth seeing fail before ruling it out.

Recommendation

A — Dock. It's the only one that survives all five sizes without losing its idea, it's built from two shapes already in the palette (no new color needed), and it's the one genuinely distinctive choice — it says "attached to something else" rather than just being a colored square, which is what C already is and D degrades into anyway at 16px. B is a reasonable second if you'd rather emphasize the read/send duality over the host/guest one.
